# 8.1 Shell soak

**Question this can falsify:** the build plan's foundation gate — that the shell,
against real webhook traffic over days rather than minutes, loses no delivery it
accepted, recovers stalled work without a webhook to wake it, binds a
configuration change on the next delivery, and can answer "what happened to
delivery X" from its own log, in observe and in dry-run. D18's business-hours
posture and the retry/dead-letter machinery are both on trial.

## Setup

- The personal sandbox App and repository from 6.1, pointed at a shell built
  from the reviewed branch, running with App credentials (live config,
  externals, resolvers). At least three calendar days, including one with no
  sandbox activity at all — the quiet day is what exercises the sweep and the
  business-hours posture rather than the happy path.
- Starts in `observe`; step 2 carries it through `dry-run` and back.
- `SWEEP_INTERVAL_SECONDS` left at its default. Store at the XDG default —
  which also exercises the state-home move on a machine with a legacy
  `packages/shell/data/` store present (expect the `legacyStoreFound` line).
- Both process streams captured to files with timestamps; the log IS the
  primary instrument, so nothing else may write to those streams.
- **The relay caveat, decided (2026-09-02): smee is acceptable for this run.**
  Direct termination needs hosting the project has deferred behind Q1/Q13, and
  operations §2 permits relays in ring-zero. Three consequences are accepted
  and recorded rather than hidden: (a) GitHub's ledger evidences smee's ack,
  not the shell's, so P9 discipline is proven store-side only (6.2 already
  proved the ledger side); (b) deliveries GitHub sends while the shell or the
  relay is down are dropped by the relay and can never reach the store — every
  planned downtime window is logged with start and end, and step 9 treats
  ledger GUIDs inside those windows as evidence for the unbuilt reconciliation
  sweep (D18's repair tool), never as soak failures; (c) step 6 uses manual
  redelivery. A future run with direct termination upgrades (a) and (b).

## Steps

1. **Baseline day.** Normal sandbox activity (issues opened, labeled, PRs).
   Once per session: pick a delivery GUID from GitHub's ledger and answer its
   whole passage from the log alone (`grep` the GUID) — accepted → claimed →
   completed, with the record kind; probe `GET /healthz` and record the answer.
   Any question the log cannot answer is a finding.
2. **Configuration binding.** Commit config changes and measure
   commit-to-binding in deliveries, not minutes: enable `intake` without
   `awaitingTriage` (expect `configRejected` / `meaningRequired` on the next
   delivery), fix it, switch `mode: dry-run` and run at least one session of
   activity there (decision reports must keep the same shape as observe —
   today's documented same-path status, recorded as observed), then
   `mode: disabled`, delete the file, restore it. The absence-memo removal
   predicts next-delivery binding in every case.
3. **Kill mid-queue.** Generate a burst, let the log show acceptance, then
   `kill -9` mid-drain. Restart. Expect: every delivery the store accepted
   before the kill ends `done` with exactly one report (D110 under a real
   crash rather than a worker-thread harness), and the interrupted claim is
   recovered by startup drain or the sweep. Log the downtime window for
   step 9.
4. **Sweep on a quiet repository.** Repeat step 3 but generate no further
   webhooks after restart. The stale claim must be requeued and completed by
   the sweep alone (`sweepRequeued` line naming the GUID, within
   `STALE_CLAIM_MINUTES` plus one interval).
5. **Poison and dead-letter.** Induce a repeatable processing failure by
   SUSPENDING the App installation (the 6.1 step-5 lever; the first draft
   named the private-key path, but the key is read once at boot and held in
   memory, so a live shell never notices — the drill falsified its own
   example). Suspension breaks every API read while the store keeps its
   backlog; sequence a burst and a kill first so durable work exists before
   the door closes. Expect the 30/60/120/240 s `deliveryAttemptFailed`
   ladder, `failed` state with payload retained after five attempts, and the
   queue behind it completing meanwhile. Unsuspend; record that the dead
   letter stays dead (no redrive verb exists — this is the evidence for
   whether one is needed).
6. **Duplicates and replays.** Use GitHub's redeliver button on a completed
   delivery (expect `deliveryDuplicate`, 202, no second report) and on the
   dead-lettered one (record what actually arrives — the dedup story for a
   redelivery under a new GUID is undecided, and this is its first evidence).
7. **Clean shutdown.** SIGTERM during a burst. Expect exit 0, one shutdown
   line, no stranded claim on restart, and the in-flight delivery either
   completed or cleanly pending — never half-recorded.
8. **Kill switch.** `KILL_SWITCH=1` for one session of activity; every
   decision must refuse with `killSwitch` and the log must say so.
9. **Close.** Reconcile ledger against store by hand: every GUID GitHub
   records for the window is terminal in the store — except GUIDs inside a
   logged downtime window, which are counted and reported as the
   reconciliation-sweep gap. `deliveryReports()` parses clean for everything
   terminal. Then two whole-window checks: at least one installation-token
   refresh visible in the run with no delivery failing across it, and — from
   GitHub's side — **zero App-authored actions on the sandbox for the entire
   window**: no comments, no label changes, no writes of any kind. The
   read-only promise, held under days of real traffic, is this protocol's
   best sentence.

## Exit criteria

The build-plan gate, made concrete: steps 3, 4, 5 and 7 pass twice on
different days; step 1's drill never fails after the first day; step 9 closes
with zero unexplained GUIDs outside logged downtime and zero App-authored
writes; and every stderr line over the window is one of the ten operator
events with a step of this protocol as its explanation.
