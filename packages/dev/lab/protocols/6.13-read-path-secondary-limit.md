# 6.13 The read-path secondary limit

The matrix has carried `REPROBE(secondary-limit-read-path)` since 6.4 measured
the write path only: a 403 with no `retry-after` at about seventy-one comment
creations at concurrency twenty. GitHub documents 900 points a minute for
REST reads and a hundred concurrent requests. Nothing here has measured either,
and the read-side plan's allowance wants to know whether a 304 costs a point.

## Question

Under an installation token, at a bounded concurrency of ten: (A) do
conditional reads answered 304 count toward the per-minute secondary limit;
(B) at what count in a minute does an unconditional read burst trip it, and
what signal arrives — status, `retry-after`, `x-ratelimit-resource`, body.

## Red line

Reads only. Concurrency ten, never a hundred. Each phase stops at the first
403, 429 or 5xx, at its cap, or at ninety seconds, and the run is not
repeated inside the hour. Phase B does not run if phase A tripped anything.
Primary cost is bounded at about 1,100 requests plus the warm-up. No token is
written anywhere.

## Method

`harness/src/run-6.13.ts`. The URL set is the sweep's own reads over the
sandbox's open items: `GET /issues/{n}`, `/issues/{n}/timeline`,
`/issues/{n}/comments`, and for pull requests `/pulls/{n}`, `/pulls/{n}/reviews`,
`/pulls/{n}/commits`. Warm-up sends each once, serially, half a second apart,
and keeps its ETag.

| Phase | Sends | Bound | What it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | conditional GETs round-robin over the set, `If-None-Match`, concurrency 10 | 600, 90 s, first signal | whether 304s count toward the per-minute points; `x-ratelimit-used` before and after |
| B | the same URLs unconditional, concurrency 10 | 1,000, 90 s, first signal | the onset count per minute and the signal's shape |

Every twenty-fifth response and every non-2xx lands in the evidence log with
its headers; each phase ends with one observation record carrying the counts,
the rate, the latency percentiles and the first signal.

## Evidence

Filled by run `2026-09-15T15-27-51-748Z`. Warm-up: 321 URLs over the sandbox's
open items, every one with an ETag (`#2`).

| Phase | Sent | Rate | Statuses | `x-ratelimit-used` | Latency p50 / max | Signal | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, conditional, concurrency 10 | 600 in 25.3 s | 1,423 / min | 590 × 304, 10 × 200 | 455 → 466 (+11: the ten 200s and the after-read) | 367 ms / 1,488 ms | none | `#595` |
| B, unconditional, concurrency 10 | 1,000 in 41.1 s | 1,459 / min | 1,000 × 200 | 467 → 1,468 (+1,001) | 368 ms / 2,123 ms | none | `#647` |

### What this run found

- **A 304 costs nothing on the primary pool** — the third confirmation (6.4
  step 2, 6.9, here) — **and nothing observable on the secondary path** at
  1,400 a minute.
- **No read-path secondary signal at 1,000 unconditional reads in 41 s at
  concurrency ten.** GitHub documents 900 points a minute for REST; a thousand
  one-point GETs inside a minute drew no 403, no 429, no `retry-after`, and no
  latency growth beyond a 2 s maximum. n=1, bounded, not repeated. The
  documented figure is not disproved — the burst may have straddled two of
  GitHub's minute windows — but nothing the sweep does approaches it: the sweep
  reads serially at a few hundred a minute at most.
- **The App's own reads are cheap and fast**: p50 under 370 ms for both phases.


## What migrates

The failure catalogue's secondary-limit row loses its `REPROBE` and gains the
read-path observation. The read-side plan's allowance takes the per-minute
figure and the 304 answer as constants with this run's citation.

## Notes

- Personal sandbox only (P8, D22). Bounded hostility: the phase-B burst is the
  read analogue of 6.4 step 4 and, like it, is stopped at the first signal and
  not repeated.
- Limits follow GitHub's
  [rate limits](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api)
  reference: 900 points a minute for REST, a GET one point, a hundred concurrent.
